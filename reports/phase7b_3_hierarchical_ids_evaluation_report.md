## 1. Title

**Controlled Hierarchical Evaluation of Two-Stage Intrusion Detection: Binary Traffic Filtering and Multiclass Attack Classification under Cryptographic Model Provenance**

---

## 2. Objective

The objective of Phase 7B.3 is to experimentally evaluate the hierarchical composition of two independently trained and blockchain-registered machine learning models:
1. **Stage 1 (Binary IDS):** Rapid binary classification distinguishing benign network flows from malicious intrusions.
2. **Stage 2 (Multiclass Attack Classifier):** Granular attack categorization classifying intrusion flows into one of nine distinct attack families.

This evaluation characterizes end-to-end classification performance, inter-stage routing dynamics, error propagation, minority class behavior, and local computational inference cost, without implementing automated defensive remediation or introducing per-flow blockchain overhead.

---

## 3. Research Question

> **How does the experimentally composed two-stage hierarchical IDS classify authentic UNSW-NB15 test flows when Stage 2 attack-category classification is conditionally invoked strictly for flows predicted as Attack by Stage 1?**

Specifically, this study answers:
1. What proportion of test flows are classified as benign by Stage 1 and bypassed around Stage 2?
2. What proportion of flows reach Stage 2, and what is the exact routing fidelity between Stage 1 predicted attacks and Stage 2 invocations?
3. What attack-category predictions are generated by Stage 2 under operational conditions?
4. How does the hierarchical binary outcome compare mathematically and empirically with the Stage 1 standalone baseline?
5. How does Stage 2 multiclass performance degrade when receiving only Stage 1-predicted attacks (operational routing) versus all true attacks (oracle routing)?
6. How many true attacks are prevented from reaching Stage 2 due to Stage 1 false negatives?
7. What is the empirical routing rate and end-to-end recall for each of the nine attack categories, especially minority classes?
8. What is the measured inference latency of the hierarchical composition in the evaluated local environment?

---

## 4. Architecture under Evaluation

```text
Network-Flow Features (42 features: 39 numerical, 3 categorical)
        │
        ▼
Stage 1: Binary IDS (ids_model.joblib, Random Forest)
        │
        ├── Benign (0) ──────────────► "Normal" (Final Output)
        │                               [Stage 2 bypassed]
        └── Attack (1)
                │
                ▼
        Stage 2: Attack Category (attack_classifier.joblib, Balanced RF)
                │
                ▼
        Attack Category (1 of 9 classes)
```

In this architecture:
- **Pre-inference gate:** Before loading either artifact, its cryptographic SHA-256 digest is recomputed and compared against the registered digest stored on the local Ethereum `ModelRegistry` smart contract.
- **Runtime inference:** Entirely decoupled from the blockchain. Zero blockchain queries are performed per flow.
- **Conditional execution:** Flows classified as Benign by Stage 1 terminate immediately with final prediction `"Normal"`, bypassing Stage 2 feature transformations and tree evaluations. Flows classified as Attack by Stage 1 are passed to Stage 2, which assigns one of the nine UNSW-NB15 attack categories.

## 7. Experimental Configurations H1/H2/H3

Three configurations were evaluated to isolate operational performance from diagnostic references:

1. **H1 — Operational Hierarchical System (Primary):**
   - Routing is governed strictly by Stage 1 predicted binary label ($\hat{y}_1 \in \{0, 1\}$).
   - If $\hat{y}_1 = 0 \rightarrow$ Output `"Normal"`.
   - If $\hat{y}_1 = 1 \rightarrow$ Pass flow to Stage 2 $\rightarrow$ Output predicted attack category $\hat{y}_2$.
   - This represents deployable two-stage inference behavior.

2. **H2 — Oracle-Routed Diagnostic Reference:**
   - Routing is governed by ground-truth binary label ($y \in \{0, 1\}$).
   - If $y = 0 \rightarrow$ Output `"Normal"`.
   - If $y = 1 \rightarrow$ Pass flow to Stage 2 $\rightarrow$ Output predicted attack category $\hat{y}_2$.
   - **Status:** *Diagnostic reference only; not deployable inference behavior.* Serves to isolate Stage 2 classifier performance in the absence of Stage 1 routing errors.

3. **H3 — Stage 1 Standalone Baseline:**
   - Established standalone binary IDS performance from Phases 1–6 ($N = 175,341$).
   - Provides historical context for binary detection capability.

---

## 8. Routing Methodology

> **Critical Methodological Assertion:**  
> Stage 2 was conditionally invoked according to Stage 1 predictions, not ground-truth labels, for the operational hierarchical evaluation (H1).

The routing invariant was verified programmatically:
$$\text{Stage 2 Routed Flows} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbb{I}(\hat{y}_{1, i} = 1) \equiv 104,893$$
Ground-truth columns `label` and `attack_cat` were segregated prior to inference and utilized strictly post-hoc for statistical metric compilation.

| **Specificity** | 97.71% | 97.71% | 0.00% |

### Binary Confusion Matrix (H1)
$$\begin{pmatrix} \text{TN} & \text{FP} \\ \text{FN} & \text{TP} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 54,718 & 1,282 \\ 15,730 & 103,611 \end{pmatrix}$$

### Architectural and Theoretical Explanation
By construction, the operational binary decision boundary of Configuration H1 is mathematically governed by Stage 1 binary routing. Because Stage 2 is conditionally invoked strictly when Stage 1 predicts an attack ($\hat{y}_1 = 1$), and Stage 2 maps routed flows exclusively to one of the nine attack categories (never to Normal), every flow where $\hat{y}_1 = 1$ remains classified as an attack at the binary level. Consequently:
$$\hat{y}_{\text{hierarchical, binary}} \equiv \hat{y}_1$$
This mathematical identity confirms implementation fidelity; it must not be conflated with an independent binary validation. Stage 2 provides value not by altering the binary boundary, but by categorizing the admitted attacks.

## 12. Oracle vs. Operational Comparison

Comparing View A (Oracle) with View B (Operational) exposes the empirical degradation introduced by upstream Stage 1 routing errors:
1. **Macro F1 Reduction:** Decreases from 53.06% (Oracle) to 51.43% on routed true attacks, and further to 50.64% when Stage 1 false positives (1,282 Normal flows) contaminate attack categories.
2. **Selective Flow Loss:** Stage 1 does not drop flows uniformly across categories; it exhibits severe class-conditional filtering, heavily impacting specific attack signatures.
3. **Contamination by Benign False Positives:** The 1,282 benign flows incorrectly admitted by Stage 1 are classified by Stage 2 into:
   - Fuzzers: 761 flows
   - Exploits: 409 flows
   - Reconnaissance: 56 flows
   - DoS: 32 flows
   - Shellcode: 20 flows
   - Generic: 4 flows  
   This inflates false alarms in these specific attack categories.

2. **Minority Classes (Analysis, Backdoor, Worms):**

## 15. Latency and Computation Measurements

Inference latency was benchmarked across $N = 100$ individual flows on the evaluated local environment (Windows x64, Intel Core i5-10300H CPU @ 2.50GHz, Python 3.12.4):

| Benchmark Stage | Target Workload | Mean Latency | Median Latency | Min Latency | Max Latency | Std Dev |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **L1: Stage 1 Alone** | Standalone binary inference | 21.04 ms | 19.85 ms | 17.58 ms | 28.23 ms | 2.52 ms |
| **L2: Stage 2 Alone** | Multiclass inference on attack flow | 23.09 ms | 22.10 ms | 19.01 ms | 31.45 ms | 2.68 ms |
| **L3: Hierarchical (Conditional)** | Stage 1 + Stage 2 if predicted attack | 32.06 ms | 35.90 ms | 17.65 ms | 48.92 ms | 10.42 ms |
| **L4: Unconditional Reference** | Both models executed on every flow | 43.31 ms | 43.12 ms | 38.12 ms | 56.78 ms | 3.89 ms |

### Latency Findings:
1. **Conditional Bypassing Advantage:** For flows predicted as Benign by Stage 1, total latency equals L1 (~20 ms). When Stage 1 predicts an Attack, total latency equals L1 + L2 (~43 ms). In the evaluated sample, L3 averaged **32.06 ms**, achieving a **26.0% computation reduction** compared to unconditional double-execution (L4: 43.31 ms).
2. **Computational Scoping:** These measurements reflect local, non-vectorized Python execution on CPU. They demonstrate that hierarchical filtering avoids invoking Stage 2 for benign flows, but no claims of "wire-speed", "real-time", or universal latency bounds are made.

## 16. Blockchain Interaction Analysis

The hierarchical evaluator verified the following architectural invariant:
$$\text{Per-Flow Blockchain Calls} \equiv 0$$

- **Pre-load Initialization Gate:** The Ethereum smart contract (`ModelRegistry.sol`) is queried once during system startup to authenticate the cryptographic SHA-256 hashes of both `ids_model.joblib` and `attack_classifier.joblib`.
- **Zero Runtime Overhead:** Runtime flow classification executes purely off-chain in local memory. Blockchain latency (mean ~80–90 ms) does not intrude into the per-flow inference path.
- **Precise Characterization:** *The evaluated per-flow hierarchical inference path performed no blockchain registry lookup.*

## 17. Security Interpretation

1. **Tamper-Evident Integrity vs. Model Safety:** Cryptographic provenance registration guarantees that the loaded model artifact precisely matches the authorized binary artifact compiled by the training authority. It prevents silent artifact tampering, unauthorized substitution, or bit-rot.
2. **No Autonomous Defense:** This research phase does NOT implement automated remediation. The system outputs predictive intelligence (Normal or Attack Category); it does not manipulate iptables, issue TCP resets, drop packets, or isolate hosts.
3. **No Inherent Security Immunity:** Provenance verification does not protect against adversarial evasion, training data poisoning present prior to digest computation, or zero-day attack vectors absent from the UNSW-NB15 training distribution.

---

## 18. Threats to Validity

1. **Dataset Representation:** Evaluated on the UNSW-NB15 benchmark, which reflects synthetic lab-generated attacks and synthesized normal traffic from 2015. Generalization to modern live IoT environments (e.g., MQTT, CoAP, Zigbee protocols) remains unverified.
2. **Feature Preprocessing Dependency:** Preprocessing pipelines are static and serialized. Protocol shifts or unseen categorical states could trigger out-of-vocabulary handling.
3. **Imbalanced Topology:** The extreme 428.89:1 imbalance between Generic and Worms in UNSW-NB15 severely bounds minority class classification despite class-weighted tree optimization.

---

## 19. Limitations

1. **Stage 1 False-Negative Bottleneck:** 74.35% of all Fuzzers flows are misclassified as Benign by Stage 1, preventing them from reaching Stage 2.
2. **Minority Category Ineffectiveness:** Analysis (2.95% recall) and Worms (15.38% recall) cannot be reliably attributed by the hierarchical pipeline.
3. **False Positive Contamination:** 1,282 benign flows reaching Stage 2 are misattributed as attacks, generating false positive alarms primarily in Fuzzers (761) and Exploits (409).
4. **Scope Limitation:** The evaluation is restricted to predictive analytics. Active remediation or dynamic packet filtering is strictly outside the current scope.

## 21. Conclusion

Phase 7B.3 successfully implemented and evaluated the controlled hierarchical composition of Stage 1 (Binary IDS) and Stage 2 (Multiclass Attack Classifier) under cryptographic model provenance.

The experimental findings confirm:
- **Operational Routing Fidelity:** Exactly 104,893 flows were routed to Stage 2, strictly matching Stage 1 attack predictions ($\Delta = 0$).
- **Decoupled Architecture:** Inference executes with zero per-flow blockchain lookups, yielding a mean conditional latency of 32.06 ms.
- **Categorical Insights:** Generic, DoS, Reconnaissance, and Shellcode achieve robust end-to-end detection (63.46%–98.06%), whereas Fuzzers is severely bottlenecked by Stage 1 false negatives (14.83% end-to-end recall), and Analysis and Worms remain constrained by extreme class imbalance.
- **Research Integrity:** Provenance verification confirms model integrity while explicitly disclaiming autonomous defense or unproven security guarantees.
